# A short guide to scrum ceremonies
## An introduction to the scrum meetings you'll see on your work calendar. Whether you're a software engineer, a technical writer, or a product manager, you'll use scrum ceremonies to plan, work on, and complete a final deliverable.

### Purpose of scrum ceremonies
Scrum ceremonies are used to plan, track, and complete various projects in a <ins>**sprint**</ins>. A sprint is a 2 week time-period during which your team works on completing portions of the final deliverable. In one quarter, there are about 6 full sprints. The main players of scrum ceremonies are as follows.
  + *Scrum master* - the facilitator who ensures teams stay on track. Helps to remove any ostacles or blockers the team may face.
  + *Product Owner (PO) or Product Manager* (PM) - the owner of a product. Acts as the subjet matter expert, or SME, for their team.
  + *Scrum team* - The team responsible for product development. Can include deveopers, technical managers, team leads, and technical writers.

### Scrum ceremony workflow
1. Sprint planning
    The team plans feature work and assigns stories to team members to work on during the sprint.
2. Daily scrum
    The team updates each other daily about their story progress and overall work progress.
3. Sprint demo
    The team showcases the work of completed stories.
4. Backlog grooming
    The team cleans up and organizes the product backlog.
5. Sprint retro
    The team reviews how the sprint went, what went well, and what could be improved.

#### Sprint planning
During sprint planning, your team plans the work targeted for the upcoming sprint. Sprint planning typically takes place the a few days before a new sprint begins. 

Prior to sprint planning, the PMs go through their backlogs to determine which user stories need to be prioritized. 

When the meeting begins, the PMs and team members discuss the details of each user story, story point it to determine the amount of time it will take to complete, and assign the stories to the appropriate team member. Once sprint planning is complete, the scrum master makes any changes to the scrum board and puts the board in the new sprint. 

#### Daily scrum
The daily scrum, or daily standup, helps ensure the team is up-to-date on the individual components being worked on by different members. It typically takes place daily and is a shorter meeting depending on the number of team members, typically about 15 to 30 minutes. Each team member gives a brief update about their progress and can bring up any blockers faced.

#### Sprint demo
The sprint demo takes place toward the end of the sprint most of the planned stories are complete. Each team member demoes their completed work and receive feedback from the broader team.

#### Sprint retrospective
The sprint retrospective also takes place toward the end of the sprint and is often combined with the demo. Team members discuss what went well during the sprint and what could be improved moving forward. The scrum master usually sends a link to a site like IdeaBoardz prior to the meeting, so that members can enter any issues they want to discuss. During the meeting, the issues are discussed and action items are made to address them.

#### Backlog grooming
Backlog grooming can take place toward the end of a sprint or quarter. The team cleans up its story backlog as needed.
